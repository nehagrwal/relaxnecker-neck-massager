How does the Relaxnecker Neck Massager work?

Some of the Relaxnecker listings refer to electrical pulse stimulation (EPM), sometimes used in product marketing, and heat. Electrical stimulation delivers a controlled pulse through the contact points to create a tingling sensation or stimulate the nerves and muscles underneath. The experience you get depends on the wave form, the intensity, the placement of the electrodes and your individual sensitivity. EPM is not a term that should be automatically equated with a medically validated TENS device.

The heat functions are designed to offer a gentle warmth around the neck. Superficial heat may help some people relax and provide temporary relief of the feeling of stiffness. But infrared branding alone doesn’t prove that a device delivers therapeutic infrared energy at a clinically effective level. More useful safety considerations are actual temperature, heat distribution, automatic shutoff, contact with skin.

Relaxnecker Neck Massager Side Effects & Risks

Electrical neck massagers can cause tingling that becomes uncomfortable, skin irritation, temporary soreness, headaches, or dizziness. Heat functions can cause redness or burns if the temperature is too high or if the device is held against the skin too long. An ill-fitting device can also put pressure on uncomfortable spots. Discontinue use if pain or irritation to skin occurs or if you experience any symptoms that are unusual.

Special care must be taken in electrical stimulation in the areas around the neck, as there are important nerves and blood vessels in this area. Do not place stimulation electrodes on the front or sides of the neck unless otherwise specifically approved for that use by a qualified clinician for that specific device. Do not use on broken, irritated or infected skin. Do not use while sleeping, driving or operating machinery. Follow the time limits and cleaning instructions of the manufacturer.”
